😴 Sleep Regressions

Baby Sleep Regressions, Ages, Signs & Survival Guide for Australian Parents

By Emma · August 2026 · 12 min read
Home › Guides › Baby Sleep Regressions

Sleep regressions are temporary, normal disruptions tied to developmental milestones , when a baby who previously slept well suddenly wakes frequently, fights naps, or is harder to settle. This guide covers every sleep regression age from 8 weeks through the toddler years, with a practical survival plan backed by Red Nose Australia, Tresillian, and Karitane.

What Is a Sleep Regression?

A sleep regression is a sudden, temporary disruption tied to a developmental leap : your baby's brain is rewiring, making it harder to settle and stay asleep. They hit at predictable sleep regression stages: around 8 weeks, 4 months, 6 months, 8–10 months, 12 months, 18 months, and 2 years. Not every baby hits every regression, and intensity varies widely. According to Karitane, nearly all resolve with time and consistent routines.

Red Nose safety first: During a sleep regression, it is tempting to bring your baby into your bed or settle them on a couch. Red Nose Australia advises that the safest place for your baby to sleep is in their own cot, on their back, with a firm mattress and no loose bedding. See Red Nose safe sleep guidelines for the full checklist.

Why Do Sleep Regressions Happen?

Sleep regressions are linked to developmental milestones. When your baby learns to roll, sit, crawl, or talk, their brain works overtime (even during sleep) disrupting sleep cycles. The Raising Children Network confirms that infant sleep pattern disruptions are a normal part of development.

  • 8 weeks: Growth spurt, increased alertness, circadian rhythm developing.
  • 4 months: Permanent change in sleep architecture (two-stage to four-stage cycles).
  • 6 months: Rolling, sitting, solids, teething, object permanence.
  • 8–10 months: Crawling, pulling to stand, separation anxiety, nap transition (3 to 2).
  • 12 months: Walking, first words, nap transition (2 to 1).
  • 18 months: Independence, language explosion, molars.
  • 2 years: Imagination, fears, potty training, big bed, final nap drop.

Signs Your Baby Is Going Through a Sleep Regression

When several of these signs of sleep regression appear together and last more than a few days, a regression is likely:

  • Suddenly waking more at night. Going from one or zero wakes to three or more, often every 45–90 minutes.
  • Fighting naps or taking shorter naps. Reliable 60–90 minute naps suddenly become 20–30 minutes.
  • Increased fussiness and clinginess. Harder to soothe, wants to be held more, difficulty settling at bedtime.
  • New developmental skills appearing. Suddenly rolling, babbling, sitting, or pulling to stand , often practising in the cot instead of sleeping.

Growth Spurt or Sleep Regression? How to Tell

Growth spurts and sleep regressions look similar but need different responses. During a growth spurt, your baby feeds more often and seems genuinely hungry . These last 2 to 7 days. During a sleep regression, feeding patterns stay the same; wakes are about comfort and practising new skills, lasting 2 to 6 weeks.

  • Feeding more often and hungrier than usual? → Growth spurt.
  • Waking but not especially hungry, just unsettled? → Sleep regression.
  • Less than a week? Give it a few more days. More than two weeks? → Regression.
  • Suddenly rolling, sitting, babbling, or pulling to stand? → Sleep regression.

The 4-Month Sleep Regression

The 4-month sleep regression is the big one : a permanent change in how your baby sleeps. Around 4 months, sleep cycles mature from deep newborn sleep to four-stage adult-like cycles. Your baby now wakes briefly between cycles, just like adults, but hasn't learned to settle back to sleep independently. If they relied on rocking, feeding, or holding at bedtime, they'll need that same help at every night wake. This regression lasts 4 to 6 weeks . Working through it lays the foundation for independent sleep.

6-Month Sleep Regression

The 6 month sleep regression hits when multiple developmental changes collide: rolling both ways, early sitting, object permanence (making separation at bedtime harder), starting solids, and teething. Signs include waking every 2–3 hours, fighting the third nap, and finding your baby stuck on their tummy after rolling.

How to handle it: Give plenty of daytime floor time to practise new skills. Maintain a consistent bedtime routine. If your baby rolls onto their tummy and can't roll back, Red Nose Australia advises it's safe to leave them (always place on back to start). Avoid introducing new sleep associations : the regression is temporary, but new habits stick. This phase lasts 2 to 4 weeks. See our 4–6 month schedule guide for age-appropriate routines.

Complete Sleep Regression Timeline: 8 Weeks to 2 Years

8-Week Sleep Regression

The first disruption : your baby becomes more alert and harder to settle. Short (1–2 weeks). Focus on natural light by day and dark, quiet nights. See our 0–3 month sleep schedule.

5-Month Sleep Regression

Often a late or extended version of the 4-month regression. The same principles apply: sleep cycles have matured, and your baby needs to learn to link them independently.

8–10 Month Sleep Regression

Driven by the mobility explosion: crawling, pulling to stand, and cruising. Separation anxiety peaks. Practise "sit back down" during daytime play and minimise night interaction. See our 7–9 month schedule guide.

11-Month Sleep Regression

Your baby is on the verge of walking and first words. Shorter but intense (2–3 weeks). Maintain routines; avoid new dependencies.

12-Month Sleep Regression

Tied to the 2-to-1 nap transition, walking, and first words. Lasts 2–4 weeks. See our 10–12 month schedule guide for nap transition help.

14-Month Sleep Regression

Hits after the first birthday with confident walking, climbing, and strong opinions. Stick with one consistent nap and hold firm on bedtime boundaries.

18-Month Sleep Regression

One of the hardest toddler regressions: language explosion, fierce independence, separation anxiety peak, and molars. Stay calm, hold boundaries kindly, and avoid power struggles. Lasts 2–6 weeks. See our toddler sleep guide.

2-Year Sleep Regression

Often the final regression. Imagination, fears, big-bed transitions, and potty training disrupt sleep. Maintain a predictable routine, use a night light, and consider keeping your toddler in the cot until they're truly ready. Lasts 2–4 weeks.

How Long Do Sleep Regressions Last?

Most sleep regressions last 2 to 6 weeks. Growth-spurt regressions: 1–2 weeks. The 4-month regression is longest (4–6 weeks : a permanent neurological change). The 6-month, 12-month, 14-month, and 2-year regressions: 2–4 weeks. The 8–10 month and 18-month regressions: 2–6 weeks. If disruption lasts beyond 8 weeks with no improvement despite consistent routines, it may not be a regression . See the "When to See a GP" section.

The ROUTINE Method: Your Sleep Regression Survival Plan

  • R — Routine. Stick to your baby's usual bedtime and nap schedule.
  • O — Offer comfort, not new habits. Patting, shushing, staying in the room . But don't create dependencies.
  • U — Understand the milestone. Naming what your baby is learning reframes the disruption as progress.
  • T — Take turns. Split night shifts. Even a 4-hour block of uninterrupted sleep helps.
  • I — Ignore the clock. Watching the minutes at 3am only increases stress.
  • N — Nap when you can. A 20-minute lie-down or earlier bedtime for yourself.
  • E — Expect it to pass. Every regression ends. This is a phase, not your new normal.

Can You Sleep Train During a Sleep Regression?

Tresillian and Karitane recommend waiting until a regression passes before starting formal sleep training. During a regression, your baby's sleep is already disrupted : adding a new method on top makes things harder. Focus on maintaining your routine and offering comfort. Gentle settling (patting, shushing, staying in the room) is fine; formal timed methods should wait. Once the regression settles (2–4 weeks), you'll see your baby's true sleep patterns clearly. See our sleep training methods guide for a full comparison of approaches.

Already sleep training when a regression hits? Pause, offer comfort, and resume once it passes. Your baby won't "unlearn" progress.

Common Mistakes Parents Make During Regressions

  1. Introducing new sleep associations. Feeding to sleep or bringing your baby into your bed might get you through the night but creates dependencies that outlast the regression.
  2. Changing the schedule too much. Dropping a nap or pushing bedtime later backfires : an overtired baby is harder to settle. See our baby sleep schedule by age guide.
  3. Assuming it will never end. Sleep deprivation makes everything feel permanent, but regressions are temporary. This is a phase; it will pass.
  4. Blaming yourself. Regressions are driven by milestones, not parenting. Your baby wakes because their brain is learning to roll, sit, or talk.
  5. Skipping safe sleep basics. Exhaustion makes it easy to cut corners. Red Nose Australia's safe sleep guidelines are even more important during a regression.

When to See a GP or Child Health Nurse

Most sleep regressions pass on their own with time and consistency, but sometimes what looks like a regression is actually something that needs medical attention. Contact your GP or child health nurse if:

  • The sleep disruption has lasted more than 8 weeks with no sign of improvement.
  • Your baby seems to be in significant pain during night wakes (arching back, pulling at ears, excessive crying that cannot be soothed).
  • You notice changes in breathing during sleep: snoring, pauses in breathing, or mouth breathing.
  • Your baby is losing weight or not feeding well during the day.
  • Your baby's crying sounds different from usual: high-pitched, weak, or unusually persistent.
  • You are struggling to cope; sleep deprivation is serious, and reaching out for support is not a sign of failure. It is what the services are there for.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the signs of sleep regression?

Suddenly waking more at night, fighting naps, increased fussiness and clinginess, and difficulty settling : all temporary.

How long does a 3-year-old sleep regression last?

Typically 2 to 4 weeks, driven by dropping the final nap, potty training, or moving to a big bed.

How to handle sleep regression?

Stay consistent with routines, offer comfort without creating new sleep associations. It will pass.

Is it normal for a 7-year-old to experience sleep regression?

Yes : triggers are usually environmental (school stress, screen time, anxiety) rather than developmental. Check for underlying issues if persistent.

Can you sleep train during a sleep regression?

Not recommended. Your baby needs extra comfort. Wait until it passes (2–4 weeks), then train from a stable baseline.

What is the difference between a growth spurt and a sleep regression?

Growth spurts involve increased hunger and last 2–7 days. Regressions last 2–6 weeks with unchanged feeding.

Is there a 6-month sleep regression?

Yes, very common : driven by rolling, sitting, starting solids, teething, and object permanence. Lasts 2–4 weeks.
