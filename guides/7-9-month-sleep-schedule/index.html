7–9 Month Baby Sleep Schedule: Wake Windows, 8-Month Regression & Nap Transitions

By Emma · Updated July 2026 · 8 min read
Home › Guides › 7–9 Month Sleep Schedule

Between 7 and 9 months, your baby's sleep is shaped by crawling, separation anxiety, and the 8-month sleep regression, all at once. This guide covers sample schedules, wake windows, the 3-to-2 nap transition, and practical tips for teething and anxiety. Written by an Australian mum following Tresillian, Karitane, and Red Nose Australia guidelines.

How Sleep Changes at 7–9 Months

Three big developmental shifts collide during this period: mobility (crawling, pulling to stand, often at 2 AM), object permanence (your baby knows you exist when you leave and doesn't like it), and mature sleep cycles (90-minute adult-like patterns). Total sleep needs: 12–14 hours per 24 hours, roughly 10–12 hours at night and 2–3 hours across daytime naps. Most babies are on 2–3 naps, with the 3-to-2 transition being the defining change of this period.

Red Nose safe sleep reminder: Once your baby can pull to stand, lower the cot mattress to the lowest setting. Remove mobiles and anything hanging above the cot. No cot bumpers, they are a suffocation and strangulation risk. For complete safe sleep guidelines, see our Safe Sleep & SIDS Prevention Guide.

7 Month Old Sleep Schedule

At 7 months, most babies are on 3 naps with wake windows of 2.25–2.75 hours. The third nap is often shrinking to a 20–30 minute catnap.

Sample 7 Month Old Sleep Schedule (3 Naps)
6:30–7:00 AMWake up, morning feed
9:00–10:15 AMNap 1 (1–1.25 hrs) wake window ~2.5 hrs
12:45–2:00 PMNap 2 (1–1.25 hrs) wake window ~2.5 hrs
4:30–5:00 PMNap 3 (catnap, 20–30 min) wake window ~2.5 hrs
6:30–7:00 PMBedtime routine, feed, settle, wake window ~2.5 hrs before bed
If your 7-month-old fights the third nap or it pushes bedtime past 7:30 PM, you may be ready to start the 3-to-2 transition. Try stretching the first two wake windows by 10–15 minutes and offering an earlier bedtime instead of the catnap.

7 Month Wake Windows & Sleep Stats

  • Wake windows: 2.25–2.75 hours (2.5 hours is the sweet spot for most)
  • Number of naps: 2–3 (3 is still typical; transitioning to 2 may begin late in the month)
  • Total day sleep: 2.5–3.5 hours across all naps
  • Night sleep: 10–12 hours with 0–2 night feeds
  • Total 24-hour sleep: 12–14 hours

8 Month Sleep Regression: Signs, Causes & How Long It Lasts

Unlike the 4-month regression (neurological), the 8-month regression is behavioural and developmental. Three things collide: separation anxiety (object permanence), motor milestones (crawling, pulling to stand), and teething (top teeth erupting). It typically lasts 2–4 weeks. If disruption continues past 4 weeks, check your wake windows and whether new sleep associations have crept in.

Signs of the 8-Month Regression

  • Suddenly waking multiple times a night after previously sleeping well
  • Fighting naps, crying, arching, refusing to settle
  • Standing in the cot and calling out instead of sleeping
  • Clinging and crying when you leave the room at bedtime
  • Short naps of 30–40 minutes instead of 60–90 minutes

How to Handle It

  • Don't introduce new sleep associations. If you start rocking or feeding to sleep during the regression, your baby will expect it after. Consistency is everything.
  • Give lots of floor time during the day. The more your baby practises crawling and pulling to stand during awake time, the less they practise at night.
  • Keep bedtime consistent (6:30–7:30 PM). Overtired babies fight sleep harder.
  • Use responsive settling. Stay in the room with a hand on your baby, then gradually reduce your involvement. See our Responsive Settling Techniques Guide for a full walkthrough.

This regression is temporary. Unlike the 4-month regression (which permanently changes how your baby sleeps), the 8-month regression passes. Your baby's sleep foundations are still there. This is a phase, not the new normal. Stay consistent and it will settle.

Wake Windows for 7, 8, and 9 Month Olds

Wake windows stretch significantly during this period. Watch for tired signs (eye rubbing, yawning, grizzling, staring) rather than rigidly following the clock.

Wake Window Progression: 7–9 Months
7 months2.25–2.75 hours. 2.5 hours is the sweet spot for most. Still on 3 naps for many babies, though the third nap is shrinking. Morning wake window is often the shortest; the one before bed is the longest.
8 months2.5–3.25 hours. Wake windows lengthen as naps consolidate. Many babies are mid-transition from 3 naps to 2. The last wake window before bed may stretch to 3–3.25 hours.
9 months2.75–3.5 hours. Most babies are firmly on 2 naps. Morning wake window is 2.75–3 hours, middle is 3–3.25 hours, and pre-bedtime can reach 3.5 hours.
These are ranges, not rules. Tresillian emphasises watching your baby's cues over watching the clock. If you consistently see overtired or undertired signs, adjust wake windows by 10–15 minutes and watch for change over 3–4 days.

9 Month Old Sleep Schedule

By 9 months, most babies are firmly on 2 naps with wake windows of 2.75–3.5 hours. Night sleep consolidates, and many babies are down to 0–1 night feeds, if you can get through the 8-month regression first.

Sample 9 Month Old Sleep Schedule (2 Naps)
6:30–7:00 AMWake up, morning feed
9:30–11:00 AMNap 1 (1–1.5 hrs) wake window ~3 hrs
2:00–3:30 PMNap 2 (1–1.5 hrs) wake window ~3 hrs
6:30–7:00 PMBedtime routine, feed, settle, wake window ~3.5 hrs before bed
This is the "3-3-4" pattern: roughly 3 hours before Nap 1, 3 hours between naps, and 3.5–4 hours before bedtime. If one nap runs short, try to extend the other or bring bedtime forward by 30 minutes.

9 Month Wake Windows & Sleep Stats

  • Wake windows: 2.75–3.5 hours (3 hours typical for the first two, up to 3.5 before bed)
  • Number of naps: 2 (the third nap should be fully dropped by 9 months for most babies)
  • Total day sleep: 2–3 hours across 2 naps
  • Night sleep: 10–12 hours with 0–1 night feeds
  • Total 24-hour sleep: 12–14 hours

The 3-to-2 Nap Transition

Most babies drop from 3 naps to 2 between 6–9 months, with 7–8 months being the most common window.

Signs Your Baby Is Ready

  • The third nap is consistently refused or takes 20+ minutes of settling
  • Bedtime pushes past 8:00 PM because of the late catnap
  • Early morning waking (before 6:00 AM) despite good naps
  • Your baby can manage a 2.5–3 hour wake window before bed

To transition, stretch the first two wake windows by 10–15 minutes and offer an earlier bedtime (6:00–6:30 PM) instead of the third nap. Expect 1–2 weeks of adjustment, some days will still need that catnap. Keep a consistent morning wake time to anchor the day.

The transition is not linear. Some days will be 2-nap days, some will still need the third nap. This doesn't mean the transition is failing, it means your baby is a human, not a clock. After 1–2 weeks of mostly 2-nap days, the transition is complete.

Teething and Sleep at 7–9 Months

Top teeth (upper central incisors) typically erupt between 8–12 months and hurt more than the bottom teeth did. Teething pain peaks for 3–5 days per tooth, not weeks. If your baby has been waking hourly for 3 weeks, it's not just teeth. Offer a cold washcloth or chilled teething ring before the bedtime feed. If using pain relief, give it 30 minutes before bedtime. Don't change your sleep routine during a teething wave, new sleep associations stick long after the tooth is through. Teething gels are not recommended by many Australian health professionals; cold pressure is safer and often just as effective.

Separation Anxiety at Bedtime

Separation anxiety peaks between 8–9 months and can turn bedtime emotional overnight. It's a sign of healthy attachment, your baby now understands object permanence and doesn't want you to leave. Play peekaboo during the day to reinforce that you come back. Keep goodbyes short and consistent. Use responsive settling, stay in the room with a hand on your baby, then gradually reduce your involvement over several nights. This phase passes. Stay consistent.

Safe Sleep at 7–9 Months (Red Nose Australia)

As your baby becomes more mobile, safe sleep focus shifts to mobility-related risks:

  • Lower the cot mattress to the lowest setting as soon as your baby can pull to stand
  • Remove mobiles and anything hanging above the cot, your baby can now reach and pull things down
  • No cot bumpers, ever. They're a suffocation and strangulation risk at every age
  • No soft toys, pillows, or loose bedding. A firm, flat mattress with a fitted sheet is all your baby needs
  • Sleep baby on their back to start each sleep period. Once they roll independently, you don't need to roll them back

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there an 8 month sleep regression?

Yes. It's driven by separation anxiety, motor milestones, and teething. Unlike the 4-month regression (neurological), this one is behavioural. It lasts 2–4 weeks and passes with consistency.

How long does the 8 month sleep regression last?

Typically 2–4 weeks. Some babies get through it in 10–14 days; others need the full month. If it continues past 4 weeks, check your wake windows and whether new sleep associations have stuck.

How do I get my 8 month old to sleep through the night?

Start with appropriate wake windows (2.5–3.25 hours), a consistent bedtime (6:30–7:30 PM), and 2–3 naps totalling 2–3 hours. Address sleep associations, if your baby needs rocking to fall asleep at bedtime, they'll need it overnight too. Tresillian's free helpline (1300 272 736) offers support for Australian families.

How much sleep does a 7 month old need?

12–14 hours total: 10–12 hours at night and 2.5–3.5 hours across 2–3 naps. Wake windows: 2.25–2.75 hours.
