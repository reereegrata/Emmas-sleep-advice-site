Newborn Sleep

0–3 Month Baby Sleep Schedule: Newborn Routines, Wake Windows & Sleep Tips

By Emma · Updated July 2026 · 8 min read
Home › Guides › 0–3 Month Sleep Schedule
← See our best swaddle picks for newborns

The first three months with your baby are beautiful, chaotic, and exhausting, often all in the same hour. If you're googling newborn sleep schedule at 2 AM while your baby treats night time like party time, you're not alone.

The honest truth: in the first 6 weeks, there is no schedule. Your newborn's sleep is driven by hunger and biology, not the clock. This guide covers newborn sleep patterns from birth to 3 months, with wake windows by week, sample routines, and practical strategies for day/night confusion, all following Red Nose Australia and Tresillian guidelines.

Newborn Sleep: What to Expect

In the first three months, your baby has no circadian rhythm and doesn't produce melatonin in a day-night pattern. Sleep happens in short bursts of 2–4 hours around the clock, your baby isn't broken, this is how newborn biology works.

  • Total sleep: 14–17 hours in a 24-hour period
  • Sleep bouts: 2–4 hours at a time, day and night
  • Night waking: Every 2–4 hours for feeding, expected and necessary
  • Active sleep: Twitching, eye movement, irregular breathing, even smiles are all normal

A newborn sleep schedule isn't something you impose. It's something that gradually emerges. Your job in the first few weeks is to learn your baby's cues, keep them safe, and survive.

Newborn Wake Windows by Week

Wake windows are how long your baby can comfortably stay awake between sleeps. In the newborn stage, these windows are incredibly short, and they include feeding time.

Newborn Wake Windows by Age (0–12 Weeks)
Weeks 0–2
30–45 minutes. Barely enough for a feed and nappy change. Don't try to keep them awake.
Weeks 2–4
45–60 minutes. Short periods of alertness after feeding, good for tummy time or face-to-face interaction.
Weeks 4–8
60–75 minutes. Longer alert periods emerge. A loose feed-awake-sleep pattern may start.
Weeks 8–12
60–90 minutes. Wake windows lengthen noticeably. Night sleep may start to consolidate.
All ranges are approximate. The most reliable guide is your baby's tired signs, not the clock. Sources: Tresillian and Raising Children Network.

Tired signs: eye rubbing, yawning, staring, jerky movements, grizzling, turning away. Catching the window before your baby becomes overtired makes settling dramatically easier.

Overtired vs undertired: An overtired baby is harder to settle, they may cry, arch their back, and seem wired. An undertired baby simply isn't ready. If settling takes more than 20 minutes, try again in 10–15 minutes.

Sample Newborn Sleep Routines

A newborn routine isn't a clock-based schedule, it's a flexible pattern following your baby's natural rhythms. Week by week, a structure starts to take shape.

Weeks 0–6: The Survival Phase

Sample Day, Newborn (0–6 Weeks)
6:00 AM
Wake and feed. Open curtains, morning light exposure. Nappy change.
6:45 AM
Tired signs appear. Swaddle and settle for sleep.
8:30 AM
Wake and feed. Short awake period, tummy time or cuddle.
9:15 AM
Settle for nap.
11:00 AM
Wake, feed, short play (nappy-free time, song).
11:45 AM
Settle for nap.
Afternoon and evening follow the same 2.5–3.5 hour feed-wake-sleep cycle. Night feeds on demand, keep the room dark, no talking, no eye contact.

Weeks 6–12: The Emerging Pattern

Sample Day, 6–12 Week Old
7:00 AM
Wake, feed, morning light. Nappy-free kick, tummy time.
8:00 AM
Tired signs. Swaddle, settle for morning nap.
10:00 AM
Wake, feed, longer awake period, outside time or floor play.
11:30 AM
Settle for midday nap.
1:00 PM
Wake, feed, play.
2:15 PM
Settle for afternoon nap.
4:00 PM
Wake, feed. Late afternoon is often the "witching hour", cluster feeding is normal.
5:30 PM
Possible short catnap or quiet time.
6:30 PM
Start bedtime routine: bath (optional), feed, swaddle, cuddle, white noise on.
7:00 PM

Night feeds still on demand (2–3 per night). Some babies stretch to 4–5 hours by 8–12 weeks, many don't, and that's fine. Adapted from Tresillian's flexible routine guidance.

Weeks 6–8: The First Shift

Around 6–8 weeks, something changes. Your baby may start sleeping slightly longer stretches at night and tired signs become more predictable. This is when melatonin production begins, social smiling emerges, and stomach capacity increases, all nudging your baby toward a loose rhythm.

Tresillian's advice: This is the ideal time to introduce a simple bedtime routine, bath, feed, swaddle, cuddle, bed, in the same order at roughly the same time each evening. Keep it short (15–20 minutes) and calm.

Months 2–3: Wake Windows & Routines

By 2–3 months, wake windows extend to 60–90 minutes and night sleep may consolidate, some babies manage one 4–5 hour stretch. Morning naps (about 1–1.5 hours after waking) are often the best nap of the day. Expect 4–5 naps per day, dropping to about 4 by 3 months. The 3-month growth spurt around 12 weeks brings temporary disruption, cluster feeding, fussiness, disrupted sleep, but it passes.

Introducing Feed-Play-Sleep

Feed-play-sleep is a simple pattern: when your baby wakes, feed first, then a short play period, then settle for sleep. This prevents the feeding-to-sleep association that becomes harder to undo at 3–4 months.

  1. Start with one cycle per day, the first morning wake-up is easiest.
  2. Feed in a bright room to reinforce day-night distinction.
  3. Even 5 minutes of play counts, tummy time, a nappy change, a song.
  4. Watch for tired signs, then settle. Swaddle, white noise, cot drowsy but awake.

In the newborn stage, cycles often look like feed-play-sleep-play-feed. It's messy, and that's fine. For more on settling techniques, see our responsive settling guide.

Day/Night Confusion

If your newborn treats 3 AM like party time, that's day/night confusion, completely normal and fixable. Most babies sort it out naturally between 6–12 weeks. Speed it up with these strategies:

  • Morning sunlight, the single strongest signal for setting the circadian rhythm.
  • Daytime = bright and active. Normal noise, talking, interaction.
  • Night time = dark and boring. Dim red night light only, no talking, no eye contact.
  • Consistent wake time, start the day at roughly the same time each morning.

Safe Sleep

Safe sleep is non-negotiable. Always place your baby on their back, on a firm flat mattress, in their own cot, with no loose bedding, pillows, or toys. Room share (not bed share) for at least 6 months. For the complete safe sleep checklist, see our Safe Sleep & SIDS Prevention Guide.

Common Newborn Sleep Challenges

Short Naps (20–30 Minutes)

Newborn naps are naturally short, 20–50 minutes is one sleep cycle. That's normal. As your baby approaches 3 months, try resettling at the first stir. Sometimes it works, sometimes it doesn't.

Only Sleeping While Held

Newborns crave contact, they just spent 9 months being held 24/7. Wait until deep sleep (limp arms, ~20 minutes) before attempting a cot transfer. Warm the cot first, keep a hand on their chest, and try again if they wake.

Noisy Newborn Sleep

Grunting, snorting, and whimpering in active sleep are normal. Wait 30–60 seconds before picking them up, many parents accidentally wake their newborns by responding to normal sleep noises.

Catnapping: Why Newborns Take Short Naps (and What to Do About It)

A catnap is a short nap lasting 20 to 30 minutes — one newborn sleep cycle. If your baby wakes 25 minutes after you put them down, you haven't done anything wrong. That's just how newborn sleep works.

Newborns have sleep cycles of 50 to 60 minutes and can't link one to the next yet. They surface into light sleep and most wake fully instead of drifting through. It's brain development, and it takes time. In the 0 to 3 month window, catnaps are entirely normal. They only become something to work on after about 4 months.

Three things that sometimes help:

  • Try resettling for 5 to 10 minutes. Patting, shushing, and a hand on the chest can help your baby drift into the next cycle. If it hasn't worked within 10 minutes, the nap is done.
  • Don't rush in at the first squeak. Newborns are noisy sleepers. Wait 30 to 60 seconds — they often settle back themselves.
  • Accept some catnaps as normal. One solid nap plus a few short ones is a perfectly fine newborn pattern.

Red Nose Australia safety note: always place your baby back in their own safe sleep space — a firm, flat cot mattress with no pillows, bumpers, or loose bedding. Never leave a sleeping baby on a couch or bouncer. For settling approaches, see our guide to responsive settling techniques.

The Witching Hour: Why Your Newborn Won't Settle in the Evening

If your baby turns into a crying, cluster-feeding mess every evening between 5 PM and 11 PM, you're in the witching hour. It affects most newborns, peaks around 6 to 8 weeks, and it's not something you've done wrong — it's a developmental phase, not a sleep problem.

The cause isn't fully understood, but a few things work together: an immature nervous system overloaded by day's end, overtiredness from short newborn naps, and cluster feeding as an instinctive way to boost milk supply.

Three ways to survive it:

  1. Cluster feed on demand. Frequent feeding during this window is biologically normal. Settle in with water, snacks, and something to watch.
  2. Babywear or do skin-to-skin. A carrier keeps your hands free and skin contact reduces cortisol. Even 20 minutes can reset the evening.
  3. Take shifts. Split the witching hour window if you have a partner. If solo, lower your expectations — order dinner, leave the washing.

The witching hour fades by 8 to 10 weeks and resolves by 12 weeks for most babies. If crying lasts more than 3 hours a day or comes with feeding difficulties, see your GP. Free help: Tresillian 1300 272 736 or Karitane 1300 227 464.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are newborn wake windows by week?

Thirty to 45 minutes at weeks 0–2, 45–60 minutes at weeks 2–4, 60–75 minutes at weeks 4–8, and 60–90 minutes at weeks 8–12. These include feeding time. The most reliable guide is your baby's tired signs, eye rubbing, yawning, staring, grizzling, not the clock.

How do I fix my newborn's day/night confusion?

Expose your baby to morning sunlight, keep daytime bright and active, keep night feeds dark and boring (no talking or eye contact), and start a simple bedtime routine from 6–8 weeks. Most babies sort this out naturally by 12 weeks.

What is the feed-play-sleep cycle for newborns?

When your baby wakes, feed first, then a short play period, then settle for sleep. The goal is to avoid feeding to sleep every time, it's messy in the newborn stage and that's fine.

How long should my newborn sleep in a 24-hour period?

Newborns typically sleep 14–17 hours total, broken into short bursts of 2–4 hours driven by hunger. If your baby is feeding well, gaining weight, and has alert periods, their sleep is likely fine.
