Sleep Regression

6-Month Sleep Regression, Signs, Causes & Survival Tips for Australian Parents

By Emma · July 2026 · 7 min read
Home › Guides › 6-Month Sleep Regression

Your baby was sleeping well. Then suddenly . at 6 months . they are waking every two hours, fighting naps, and nothing you try seems to work. Welcome to the 6-month sleep regression. It is real, it is common, and it will pass.

At 6 months, your baby's brain is going through an enormous developmental leap: rolling, sitting, starting solids, teething, and discovering object permanence. All of this disrupts sleep. This guide explains why it happens, what to expect, and how to get through it: with Australian-specific advice from Tresillian, Karitane, and Red Nose Australia.

Is There a 6-Month Sleep Regression?

Yes . absolutely. While the 4-month regression changes how your baby sleeps (permanently altering sleep cycles), the 6-month regression is about what your baby can do. Rolling onto their tummy but not back, sitting up in the cot, reaching for objects . these new skills are so exciting they override the drive to sleep. Add teething, starting solids, and separation anxiety, and you have a perfect storm. The good news: it is temporary, typically lasting 2 to 4 weeks. See our complete sleep regression guide for the full age-by-age picture.

What Causes the 6-Month Sleep Regression?

Unlike the 4-month regression (a neurological rewiring), the 6-month regression is driven by physical and cognitive milestones:

  • Object permanence. Your baby now understands you exist even when they cannot see you . and they want you back. This is a huge cognitive leap but makes self-settling harder.
  • Rolling and sitting. Many 6-month-olds roll onto their tummy but cannot roll back, then cry for help.
  • Starting solids. New foods can cause digestive discomfort . wind, constipation, or reflux flare-ups disrupt sleep.
  • Teething. The first teeth often emerge around 6 months. Pain peaks at night when there is no daytime distraction.
  • Separation anxiety. Your baby realises you are a separate person. Bedtime becomes a separation event, triggering strong protests.

6-Month Sleep Regression Signs

  • Suddenly waking every 1 to 3 hours after previously sleeping longer stretches
  • Fighting naps . taking 30 minutes or more to settle
  • Practising new skills in the cot . rolling, rocking on all fours, sitting up
  • Increased clinginess and crying at bedtime
  • Shorter naps (20 to 30 minutes instead of 60 to 90)

If you are also seeing flushed cheeks, drooling, and chewing on everything, teething is likely part of the picture.

How Long Does the 6-Month Sleep Regression Last?

Most 6-month sleep regressions last 2 to 4 weeks. If it persists beyond 6 weeks with no improvement, it may not be a regression . check for an underlying schedule issue or see the doctor section below.

Teething or Sleep Regression? How to Tell

  • Teething: Flushed cheeks, swollen gums, excessive drooling, chewing hands constantly. Discomfort is worse when lying flat. Usually lasts 3 to 7 days.
  • Regression: Practising skills (rolling, sitting), calling out for you, resisting sleep rather than showing pain. Lasts 2 to 4 weeks.
  • Both at once: Very common. Treat the teething pain (cold teether, paracetamol if GP-approved) but keep sleep routines consistent.

6-Month Sleep Schedule and Wake Windows

A well-timed schedule reduces regression severity. At 6 months, most babies need:

7:00 AM . Wake, breast/bottle feed

9:00 AM . Nap 1 (1 to 1.5 hours)

12:00 PM . Wake, solids then milk feed

1:30 PM . Nap 2 (1 to 1.5 hours)

4:00 PM . Wake, snack feed

6:00 PM . Bath, bedtime routine

6:30 PM . Final feed, into cot awake

7:00 PM . Asleep

Typical: 2 to 3 naps (2.5 to 3.5 hours total). Wake windows: 2 to 2.5 hours. See our 4 to 6 month schedule guide.

6 Tips to Handle the 6-Month Sleep Regression

  1. Practise skills during the day. Give your baby floor time to master rolling and sitting. Less night-time practising.
  2. Do not introduce new sleep associations. Feeding to sleep or rocking for ages creates dependencies. Comfort is fine; new habits are not.
  3. Stick to the routine. A consistent bedtime ritual signals sleep time even when your baby's brain is busy. Consistency wins every regression.
  4. Manage teething pain. Cold teether before bed, paracetamol if GP-approved. See Raising Children Network for safe teething advice.
  5. Early bedtime if naps fail. If naps are short, bring bedtime forward 30 minutes. An overtired baby is harder to settle.
  6. Tag-team with your partner. Even a 4-hour block of uninterrupted sleep builds resilience. Split shifts if you can.

Can You Sleep Train During the 6-Month Regression?

Tresillian and Karitane recommend waiting. Your baby is going through a rapid developmental phase . adding formal sleep training on top can increase distress. Focus on comfort and routine. Once the regression passes, you will see your baby's true sleep patterns and can train from a stable baseline. See our sleep training methods guide.

When to Call a Doctor

Contact your GP or child health nurse if:

  • Sleep disruption lasts more than 6 weeks with no improvement
  • Your baby seems in significant pain beyond normal fussing
  • You notice breathing changes during sleep: snoring, pauses, mouth breathing
  • Your baby is losing weight or feeding poorly
  • You are struggling to cope . reaching out is not a failure

Australian support: Tresillian (1300 272 736), Karitane (1300 227 464), Pregnancy Birth and Baby (1800 882 436).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the 6-month sleep regression real?

Yes. It is tied to major developmental leaps . rolling, sitting, object permanence, teething. Nearly every baby goes through it.

How long does the 6-month regression last?

Typically 2 to 4 weeks. Beyond 6 weeks, check for schedule issues or teething.

Can teething cause sleep regression at 6 months?

Yes . teething pain peaks at night. Treat the pain but maintain sleep routines.

How many naps should a 6-month-old take?

Two to three naps per day, with wake windows of 2 to 2.5 hours. Total daytime sleep: 2.5 to 3.5 hours.

Should I feed my baby every time they wake?

Not necessarily. At 6 months, most babies can go 4 to 6 hours without a feed at night. Try settling without feeding first.
