4–6 Month Sleep

4–6 Month Baby Sleep Schedule: Wake Windows, Regressions & Nap Transitions

By Emma · Updated July 2026 · 11 min read
Home › Guides › 4–6 Month Sleep Schedule
← See our best baby sleeping bags for 4-6 months

If the newborn stage was about survival, 4–6 months is about change. Sleep cycles mature, naps consolidate, and you might be in the thick of the infamous 4-month sleep regression. It's a lot. But it's also the stage where real routines start to take shape.

This guide gives you wake windows, sample schedules, and regression survival tips for 4–6 months, fast. All recommendations follow Red Nose Australia, Tresillian, and Karitane guidelines.

The 4-Month Sleep Regression: Survival Guide

The 4-month regression is a permanent neurological change: your baby's sleep cycles mature from newborn (50-minute) to adult-like (90-minute) cycles. They now surface into light sleep between cycles and may struggle to link them. It typically lasts 2–6 weeks.

Signs It's the Regression

  • Waking every 45 min–2 hours overnight (previously slept longer)
  • Fighting naps, 20+ minutes to settle when they used to go down easily
  • Naps shortening to 30–45 minutes (one sleep cycle) with no resettling
  • Increased fussiness and clinginess during the day

Survival Tips

  • Don't introduce new sleep associations. If you start feeding or rocking to sleep now, expect that pattern to stick long after the regression passes. Your baby needs to learn how to link sleep cycles on their own.
  • Keep your routine consistent. Same bedtime, same settling approach, same sleep environment. Consistency is your anchor through this storm.
  • Try responsive settling. Stay in the room, offer comfort without picking up, and gradually reduce your involvement over several nights. See our Responsive Settling guide.
  • Watch wake windows. An overtired baby fights sleep harder. At 4 months, wake windows are 1.5–2.5 hours. If your baby has been awake for 2.5 hours, settling will be much harder.

When to check with your GP: If the regression lasts longer than 6 weeks with no improvement, or if your baby seems in pain (not just fussy). Sometimes what looks like a prolonged regression is actually reflux, a food intolerance, or an ear infection.

4-Month Sleep Schedule: Wake Windows & Routine

At 4 months, wake windows are 1.5–2.5 hours. Most babies take 4 naps. Watch tired signs, eye rubbing, yawning, grizzling, not the clock.

  • First wake window (morning): 1.5–1.75 hours
  • Midday wake windows: 1.75–2.25 hours
  • Last wake window (before bed): 2–2.5 hours
  • Naps: 4 per day · Night sleep: 10–12 hours with 0–2 feeds
Sample 4-Month Sleep Schedule (4 Naps)
7:00 AMWake, nappy change, feed
8:30 AMNap 1 (45 min–1.5 hrs), dark room, white noise
10:00 AMWake, feed, play: tummy time, song or book
11:45 AMNap 2 (45 min–1.5 hrs)
1:15 PMWake, feed, play: floor time, outside walk
3:00 PMNap 3 (45 min–1.5 hrs)
4:15 PMWake, feed, quiet play
5:30 PMNap 4: catnap (20–30 min max). Keep short to protect bedtime.
6:00 PMWake, bedtime routine: bath, feed, book, cuddle
7:00–7:30 PMBedtime: into cot drowsy but awake, dark room, white noise
Flexible guide, not rigid rules. If the fourth nap becomes a battle, your baby may be ready to drop to 3 naps, see nap transition below.

5-Month Sleep & Sample Schedule

Wake windows stretch to 2–2.5 hours and most babies drop to 3 naps. Night sleep should be consolidating, many 5-month-olds can manage a 6–8 hour stretch without a feed. Rolling is the big milestone at this age. Your baby may roll onto their tummy in the cot overnight and wake themselves up. Give plenty of daytime floor practice. Stop swaddling immediately once your baby can roll and switch to an arms-out sleeping bag. A brief sleep disruption (1–2 weeks) is common around this age from rolling and increased awareness, stay consistent and it will pass.

Sample 5-Month Sleep Schedule (3 Naps)
7:00 AMWake, feed, nappy change
9:00 AMNap 1 (1–1.5 hrs)
10:30 AMWake, feed, play
12:30 PMNap 2 (1–1.5 hrs)
2:00 PMWake, feed, play
4:00 PMNap 3 (30–45 min), catnap to bridge to bedtime
4:45 PMWake, feed, quiet wind-down
6:30 PMBedtime routine: bath, feed, book, cuddle
7:00–7:30 PMBedtime: into cot drowsy but awake
If the gap between Nap 3 and bedtime feels long, bring bedtime forward 15–30 minutes rather than adding a fourth nap back in.

6-Month Sleep Schedule: Wake Windows & Naps

Wake windows reach 2–3 hours. Most 6-month-olds take 3 solid naps and need 12–15 hours total sleep. Around this age, object permanence emerges, your baby now knows you exist when you leave the room, which can spike separation anxiety at bedtime. Daytime peekaboo games and a consistent routine help. This disruption typically lasts 2–4 weeks.

If your baby fights the third nap consistently for 2+ weeks, they may be ready to drop to 2 naps. Average age for the 3-to-2 transition is 7–9 months.

The 4-to-3 Nap Transition

Most babies drop from 4 naps to 3 between 4–5 months. Signs: the fourth nap becomes a battle, bedtime pushes past 8:30 PM, or your baby can comfortably manage a 2-hour wake window before bed.

How to do it: Stretch wake windows by 10–15 minutes across the day, drop the fourth nap, and bring bedtime forward to 6:00–6:30 PM during the transition. Expect it to take 1–2 weeks, some days they'll need four naps, others three. That's normal.

Early bedtime is your best tool. A 6:00 PM bedtime during a nap transition is strategy, not failure. An overtired baby fights sleep harder and wakes more. Tresillian recommends earlier bedtimes during nap transitions as standard practice.

Starting Solids and Sleep

Around 6 months, starting solids can affect sleep, some babies settle better with a fuller tummy, others have temporary digestive disruption. Introduce new foods at lunchtime (not dinner) so you spot reactions during the day. Leave at least 45 minutes between solids and naps. Don't expect solids to magically fix sleep, developmental changes and sleep associations matter more than hunger at this age.

Safe Sleep at 4–6 Months

Red Nose Australia guidelines: always place baby on their back, firm flat mattress, no loose bedding or cot bumpers, room share for at least 6–12 months. Stop swaddling at the first sign of rolling, transition to an arms-out sleeping bag. If your baby rolls onto their tummy independently during sleep, you don't need to roll them back as long as the cot is clear of hazards. Full safe sleep checklist →

What are the wake windows for a 4-month-old?

1.5–2.5 hours. The first window is shortest (1.5–1.75 hrs), the last before bed is longest (2–2.5 hrs). Most 4-month-olds take 4 naps and need 12–15 hours total sleep. Follow tired signs over the clock.

When should my baby drop from 4 naps to 3?
