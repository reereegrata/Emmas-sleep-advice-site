👶 Toddler Sleep

Toddler Sleep Guide: 12–36 Month Schedules, Regressions & Bedtime Tips for Australian Parents

By Emma · Updated August 2026 · Last reviewed: October 2026
Home › Guides › Toddler Sleep Guide

You made it through the newborn haze, survived the 4-month regression, and navigated the 8-10 month separation anxiety. Now your baby is a toddler, walking, talking, and fighting bedtime like they are personally offended by the concept of sleep. This guide covers everything Australian parents need to know about toddler sleep from 12 to 36 months: age-based schedules, the 2-to-1 nap transition, sleep regressions at 12, 18, and 24 months, bedtime battles, the cot-to-bed move, and safe sleep guidelines from Red Nose Australia. The schedules below follow wake windows that work for most toddlers, but every child is different. Looking for the first year? Our complete schedule guide covers 0-12 months. If your toddler is happy and alert when they are awake, you are probably on the right track.

Toddler Sleep Needs: What to Expect from 12–36 Months

Between 12 and 36 months, toddlers need 11 to 14 hours of total sleep in a 24-hour period, according to the Raising Children Network. This breaks down into 10 to 12 hours overnight and a daytime nap of 1 to 2 hours. The 2-to-1 nap transition happens between 13 and 18 months for most children, and it rarely goes smoothly.

How Toddler Sleep Differs From Baby Sleep

By 12 months, your toddler's circadian rhythm is mature. They produce melatonin at the right times, their sleep cycles have lengthened to about 90 minutes (similar to an adult's), and they can connect sleep cycles without help, if they have learned how. The challenge shifts from biology to behaviour. Toddlers now have object permanence (they know you exist even when you leave the room), language skills (they can call out for you by name), and opinions. Strong ones. About everything, including bedtime.

Wake Windows by Age

Wake windows stretch as your toddler grows. At 12 months, most toddlers can handle 3 to 4 hours of awake time between sleeps. By 18 months, that extends to 4.5 to 5.5 hours. By 2 years, many can manage 5 to 6 hours before needing a nap or bedtime. The thing to remember: a toddler who is under-tired will fight sleep. A toddler who is over-tired will also fight sleep, but louder. Watch for eye-rubbing, zoning out, or crankiness as your cues.

Sleep needs by age (Raising Children Network): 12-15 months: 11-14 hours total (10-12 night, 1-2 nap). 15-24 months: 11-14 hours total (10-12 night, 1-2 nap). 2-3 years: 10-13 hours total (10-12 night, 0-1 nap). If your toddler is within an hour of these ranges and happy during the day, the exact numbers are not worth losing sleep over.

12–15 Month Sleep Schedule: Two Naps & Wake Windows

At 12 to 15 months, your toddler is likely still on two naps, though the afternoon nap may be getting shorter and harder to settle. Wake windows sit around 3 to 4 hours. The 2-to-1 nap transition often starts showing signs during this window: your toddler may start refusing the afternoon nap or taking 45 minutes to fall asleep for it. If this happens consistently for two weeks or more, they may be ready to drop to one nap. If you are moving on from the 10-12 month schedule, that guide covers the nap structure that leads into this stage.

Typical 12–15 Month Wake Windows

  • Morning wake window: 3 to 3.5 hours
  • Midday wake window: 3.5 to 4 hours
  • Bedtime wake window: 3.5 to 4 hours

Night wakings are still common at this age. Separation anxiety peaks again around 12 months, and motor milestones like walking and climbing can send sleep sideways for a week or two. If your toddler wakes at night, keep responses short and boring: a quick reassurance, a pat, leave. The less interesting the night waking, the faster it passes. If you are still watching a monitor at this age, our guide on when it's time to stop using the baby monitor covers when it becomes optional.

Sample 12–15 Month Sleep Schedule (2 Naps)
6:30-7:00 AMWake up, morning milk feed. Open curtains so natural light anchors the body clock.
7:30 AMBreakfast, solids with water. Offer finger foods like toast, fruit, eggs.
10:00 AMMorning nap, 60-90 minutes. Do not let it run past 11:30 AM or it will push the afternoon nap too late.
11:30 AMWake, morning tea, small snack, then active play. Walking practice, park, or playgroup.
12:30 PMLunch, solids. Include protein and veg.
2:30 PMAfternoon nap, 45-60 minutes. Must finish by 4:00 PM to protect bedtime. This nap often shortens first.
3:30 PMWake, afternoon snack, then quiet play or outdoor time.
5:30 PMDinner, solids. Keep it calm. No screens in the hour before bed.
6:30 PMBath, books, wind-down. Dim lights. This routine tells your toddler sleep is coming.
7:00 PMFinal milk feed, brush teeth, into cot awake. White noise, dark room.
Total day sleep: 2-2.5 hours. Total night sleep: 10.5-11.5 hours. Wake windows: ~3h / 3.5h / 3.5-4h. If the afternoon nap is consistently refused for 2+ weeks, your toddler may be ready for one nap. See the 15-18 month schedule below.

12-month sleep regression: This regression is driven by walking, first words, and a fresh wave of separation anxiety. It usually lasts 1-3 weeks. Do not introduce new sleep props during a regression. Respond briefly, keep lights off, and give plenty of daytime practice time for new motor skills. If it drags past 3 weeks, the issue is probably a schedule that needs tweaking rather than the regression itself.

15–18 Month Sleep Schedule: One Nap & The 2-to-1 Transition

By 15 to 18 months, most toddlers have dropped to one nap. If your toddler is still on two naps at 15 months and they are working (both naps settling easily, bedtime not creeping past 8:30 PM), there is no rush to drop the second nap. The transition window is 13 to 18 months, and every child gets there in their own time.

Signs Your Toddler Is Ready for One Nap

  • The afternoon nap is a battle. It takes 30+ minutes to settle or gets refused outright for two weeks or more
  • The afternoon nap pushes bedtime past 8:30 PM and your toddler still wakes early
  • Your toddler handles a longer morning wake window (4+ hours) and takes one solid nap
  • Night sleep is disrupted with split nights or early waking despite two decent naps

During the transition, you will have some two-nap days and some one-nap days. On one-nap days, offer an early bedtime (as early as 6:00 PM if needed) to bridge the gap. Overtired toddlers sleep worse, not better. Keeping them up late to build sleep pressure backfires almost every time.

Typical 15–18 Month Wake Windows

  • Morning wake window: 4.5 to 5 hours
  • Afternoon wake window: 5 to 5.5 hours

Night wakings at this age are often linked to the 18-month sleep regression, which can start as early as 15-16 months. Language explosions, independence battles, and separation anxiety all peak around now. A consistent bedtime routine is your best defence: same order every night, same calm vibe.

Sample 15–18 Month Sleep Schedule (1 Nap)
6:30-7:00 AMWake up, morning milk feed. Open curtains for morning light.
7:30 AMBreakfast, solids with water. Keep it filling: porridge, toast, eggs, fruit.
9:00 AMMorning tea, small snack if hungry, then active play or outing.
11:30 AMLunch, solids. Eat before nap so a full belly does not cut the nap short.
12:00-12:30 PMNap, 1.5-2.5 hours. Dark room, white noise. Do not cap unless it runs past 3:00 PM.
2:00-3:00 PMWake, afternoon snack, then outdoor time or play. Run off the energy.
5:30 PMDinner, solids. Family meal if possible.
6:15 PMBath, books, quiet play. Dim lights, no screens. Consistent wind-down.
6:45 PMFinal milk feed, brush teeth, into cot awake.
7:00-7:30 PMBedtime. Dark room, white noise. The goal: asleep within 15-20 minutes of going into the cot.
Total day sleep: 1.5-2.5 hours. Total night sleep: 10.5-11.5 hours. Wake windows: ~4.5-5h / 5-5.5h. On two-nap transition days, pull bedtime earlier if the nap was short. An overtired toddler at 7:30 PM is much harder to settle than one going down at 6:30 PM.

Early waking (before 6 AM): If your toddler consistently wakes before 6 AM and is not hungry, check the room first. Light creeping through curtains is the most common culprit. Blackout blinds help, and leaving them in the cot until 6 AM (even if they are awake and chatting) teaches the body clock that this is still night time. Also check the nap schedule: an overly long nap or a bedtime that is too early can push wake time earlier in toddlers over 15 months.

18–24 Month Sleep Schedule & The 18-Month Regression

By 18 months, most toddlers are down to one midday nap and need 11 to 14 hours of total sleep in 24 hours, according to the Raising Children Network. Wake windows stretch to about 4.5 to 5.5 hours, and by 24 months many toddlers handle close to six hours before bed. Protect the nap and bedtime together: a nap that runs past 3:00 PM pushes bedtime later.

Typical 18-24 Month Wake Windows

  • Morning wake window: 4.5 to 5 hours
  • After-nap wake window: 5 to 5.5 hours
  • By 24 months: up to 6 hours before bed
Sample 18-24 Month Sleep Schedule (1 Nap)
6:30-7:00 AMWake up, open the curtains.
7:30 AMBreakfast, then play and time outside.
9:30 AMMorning tea, then active play or an outing.
11:45 AMLunch, a full meal before the nap.
12:30 PMNap, 1.5 to 2.5 hours. Wake by 3:00 PM.
2:30 PMWake, snack, then outdoor play.
5:30 PMDinner, then a calm wind-down.
7:00 PMBedtime, calm and drowsy.
Day sleep: 1.5-2.5 hours. Night sleep: 10.5-11.5 hours. After a short nap, bring bedtime forward 15-30 minutes.

What the 18-Month Regression Looks Like

Around 18 months, a vocabulary explosion, a push for independence and a fresh wave of separation anxiety land together. The Raising Children Network notes that bedtime resistance is the most common sleep problem parents report, and that it peaks around 18 months. Expect two to six weeks of longer settling or extra night calls. Keep the routine identical, keep night check-ins short and dull, and do not add new sleep props. If white noise has become part of the routine and you want to fade it out, our guide on how to wean off white noise sets out a gradual four-week method. Give new skills plenty of daytime practice so the brain is not rehearsing them at 2:00 AM.

2–3 Year Sleep Schedule & Dropping the Last Nap

Between two and three, most children need 12 to 13 hours of sleep in 24 hours: about 11 hours overnight plus a one to two hour nap. Tresillian puts the range at 12 to 13 hours a day for children aged 12 months to three years, and notes some children stop napping as early as two while others still nap until they start school.

Signs Your Toddler Is Ready to Drop the Last Nap

  • The nap takes 30 minutes to settle, or is refused most days for two to three weeks
  • Bedtime has crept past 8:30 PM and they still wake at the usual time
  • A skipped nap causes no meltdown by dinner
  • Night waking or split nights continue despite a consistent routine

One skipped nap is not a sign; look for a two-week pattern.

How to Drop the Nap Without Losing the Afternoon

Shorten the nap by 15 to 30 minutes every few days rather than removing it at once. Keep the same time slot as quiet time: 45 to 60 minutes in their room with books or puzzles. Bring bedtime forward 15 to 30 minutes on no-nap days, and expect two to three weeks of wobble.

Sample 2-3 Year Sleep Schedule (Nap or Quiet Time)
6:45-7:15 AMWake up, open the curtains.
7:45 AMBreakfast, then play and time outside.
12:00 PMLunch, then a calm story.
12:45 PMNap or quiet time, 60-90 minutes of sleep or 45-60 minutes of quiet play.
2:30 PMWake, snack, then active play.
5:30 PMDinner, then a slow wind-down.
7:00-7:30 PMBedtime, same routine whether they napped or not.
On no-nap days, aim for a 6:30 PM bedtime. Night sleep: 11-12 hours.

Toddler Sleep Regressions: 12, 18 & 24 Months Explained

A toddler sleep regression is a stretch of two to six weeks when a child who was sleeping well starts fighting sleep, waking more, or both. There is no medical diagnosis; the word describes a cluster of developmental changes landing at once: new movement, new words, new fears, and a stronger sense of self.

12 Months: Walking, Words and the Nap Shift

First steps, growing vocabulary, and separation anxiety arrive together, and the 2-to-1 nap transition often starts here. Expect two to four weeks. Give them floor time to practise walking, keep night responses short and dull, and pull bedtime earlier after a short nap.

18 Months: Language, Independence and the Bedtime Standoff

This is usually the loudest. Your toddler understands far more than they can say, wants to do everything themselves, and has opinions about bedtime. The Raising Children Network notes that bedtime resistance peaks around 18 months. Expect two to six weeks. Offer two choices inside the routine, hold the order fixed, and respond once, then leave.

24 Months: Imagination, Fears and Big Changes

At two, imagination takes off, bringing fear of the dark and monsters. Cot-to-bed, toilet training, a new sibling, or childcare can land together. Expect three to six weeks. A dim night light, a familiar comforter, and a check for scary shadows help.

When It Is Not a Regression

Look for another cause if the change came with a fever, ear pulling, a new cough, or appetite change. Teething, illness, hunger, a room that is too light, too much day sleep, or a slipped bedtime are more common than a regression. If sleep is still worse after six weeks, or you are worried about breathing, growth, or mood, see your GP.

Bedtime Battles & The Toddler Stall (And How to Win)

Bedtime resistance is the most common sleep problem parents report, and it peaks around 18 months before easing as language grows, according to the Raising Children Network. The behaviour is normal. Your toddler is testing whether the boundary holds, wants to stay with the family, and has just enough imagination to wonder what happens after you leave the room.

Why the Stall Works

One more book. A drink of water. A sudden need for the toilet. "I have to tell you something." Each trip back out of bed buys attention, and attention is the reward. If every request turns into a conversation, the stall is working exactly as designed. The fix is not a stricter child; it is a less interesting response.

A Routine That Ends the Negotiation

  • Keep the routine to 30 to 40 minutes, which is the length Tresillian recommends
  • Same order every night: bath, pyjamas, teeth, two books, cuddle, bed
  • Offer choices inside the routine, such as which book or which pyjamas
  • Call water and toilet once, before bed, and mean it
  • Put your toddler down calm and drowsy, not asleep
  • When they appear, walk them back without a chat and repeat
  • Praise them the next morning when they stay in bed

Check the sleep pressure first: if bedtime has become a 90-minute negotiation, try moving bedtime 15 minutes later for one week. If your toddler is melting down by 6:00 PM instead, they are overtired and bedtime needs to move earlier, not later.

Cot-to-Bed Transition: When, How & What to Expect

Red Nose Australia's position is clear: a cot that meets Australia's mandatory infant sleep product standards is safer while your child fits it and cannot climb out. Move them when they can climb out or look likely to succeed, when they reach the cot's height or weight limit, when the side no longer contains them, or when they need the toilet overnight. That usually happens between two and three and a half years, but it can be as early as 18 months.

Preparing the Room Before the First Night

  • Anchor chests of drawers, wardrobes, bookcases and TVs to the wall
  • Secure blind and curtain cords out of reach
  • Lock windows or fit restrictors, and gate the stairs
  • Lock away medicines, cleaning products, button batteries and small choking hazards
  • Keep heaters, power points and electrical cords out of reach
  • Keep the bed away from the wall so your child cannot become trapped in the gap

Choosing the Bed

A toddler bed or a firm mattress on the floor is the safer first step, because it reduces the distance your child can fall. The mattress must be firm, flat and the right size for the frame, with no gaps. Check that any bed rail sits tight against the mattress. Wait until your child is over two and out of the cot before introducing a pillow, and do not use weighted blankets. Bunk beds are not recommended under nine years.

The First Week

Expect extra visits. Walk your toddler back to bed calmly, tuck them in, say goodnight, and keep the words short. Praise them when they stay in the big bed. Tresillian suggests letting your toddler climb into bed themselves and keeping their comforter close, which makes the new bed feel familiar.

Safe Sleep for Toddlers: Red Nose Australia Guidelines

SIDS is very rare after the first year, but Red Nose Australia's safe sleep principles do not stop at 12 months. The risks that remain are different: falls from a bed, entrapment in gaps, soft bedding covering the face, and furniture that can topple once your child is mobile.

In the Cot

  • Use a cot that meets the mandatory Australian standard and assemble it exactly as instructed
  • Use only the mattress designed for that cot: firm, flat, and no gaps
  • Keep pillows, bumpers, soft toys and loose objects out, and nothing to stack as a step
  • Lower the mattress base once your child can sit, pull up, or stand
  • Keep blind cords, mobiles, heaters and electrical items well out of reach

Overheating and Room Temperature

Red Nose does not recommend a specific room temperature, because no evidence shows a set temperature prevents sudden infant death. Dress your toddler for the room, keep their head and face uncovered, and check their back or tummy: it should feel warm, not hot or clammy. The 18 to 22 degree range many Australian parents use is a comfort guide, not a Red Nose target. If you use a sleeping bag, match the TOG to the room and layers underneath.

After the Move to a Bed

Use a firm, flat mattress with no gaps around the frame, and check that any bed rail fits tightly. A pillow is only appropriate after age two, once out of the cot, and weighted blankets are not safe for toddlers. Anchor furniture and keep the path to the door clear. For the full checklist, read our safe sleep and SIDS prevention guide for Australia. Red Nose's Safe Sleep Advice Line is 1300 998 698.

Frequently Asked Questions About Toddler Sleep

How much sleep does a toddler need?

Toddlers aged 12 to 36 months need 11 to 14 hours of total sleep in 24 hours, according to the Raising Children Network. That is usually 10 to 12 hours overnight and a nap of one to two hours.

When should I move my toddler from a cot to a bed?

Red Nose Australia says to move when your child can climb out or looks likely to succeed, not at a set age. That is usually between two and three and a half years, though it can start at 18 months.

How many naps should my toddler have?

Most toddlers move from two naps to one between 13 and 18 months. By 18 months, one nap of one to two hours is typical; by three, many children are on one nap or none. Replace the dropped nap with quiet time.

Why is my toddler waking at 5:00 AM?

Early waking is usually light, too much day sleep, or a bedtime too early for the nap they take. Blackout blinds, a nap ending by 3:00 PM, and a bedtime between 6:30 and 7:30 PM fix most cases.

Are night terrors the same as nightmares?

No. Night terrors affect about 1 in 20 children and are most common between two and four years, according to the Raising Children Network. They happen in the first hours of sleep; the child stays asleep and does not remember. Nightmares happen later and are remembered.

When should I get help?

See your GP or child health nurse if sleep problems last over six weeks, affect the whole family, or come with feeding, breathing, growth, or mood concerns. Tresillian's Parent Help Line is 1300 272 736; Karitane's Careline is 1300 227 464.
