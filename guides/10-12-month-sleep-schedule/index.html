10–12 Month Sleep

10–12 Month Baby Sleep Schedule, Wake Windows, 11-Month Regression & Pre-Toddler Sleep

By Emma · Updated July 2026 · 8 min read

You've almost made it through the first year. At 10–12 months, your baby is cruising along furniture, maybe taking first steps, and developing a proper personality, but sleep can get bumpy again right when you thought you had it sorted.

This guide covers wake windows, nap schedules, and regression survival for 10, 11, and 12 months, with sample routines following Red Nose Australia, Tresillian, and Karitane guidelines.

How Sleep Changes at 10–12 Months

At 10–12 months, your baby's sleep is shaped by motor milestones (pulling to stand, cruising, first steps), language development, and changing sleep needs. Most still need 12–14 hours total sleep across two naps and overnight, with wake windows of 3–4 hours. Some show early signs of the 2-to-1 nap transition, though most aren't ready until 13–18 months. 10-Month Sleep Schedule: Wake Windows & Naps

At 10 months, most babies are firmly on two naps with wake windows of 3–3.5 hours. Balance is key, enough awake time to build sleep pressure, but not so much that your baby becomes overtired.

Here is a typical 10-month sleep schedule:

Sample 10-Month Sleep Schedule (2 Naps)
6:30–7:00 AMWake up, morning feed. Open curtains for morning light.
7:30 AMBreakfast, solids (porridge, toast, fruit). Offer water.
9:30–10:00 AMMorning nap, 1–1.5 hours. Cap at 60–75 min if it pushes afternoon nap late.
11:00–11:30 AMWake, feed, breastmilk or formula, then floor play.
12:00 PMLunch, solids. Finger foods, soft veg, proteins.
2:00–2:30 PMAfternoon nap, 1–1.5 hours. Must end by 4:00 PM.
3:30–4:00 PMWake, feed, breastmilk or formula. Snack if hungry.
5:30 PMDinner, solids. Keep it relaxed.
6:15 PMBath, quiet play, wind-down. Dim lights, read a book.
6:45 PMFinal feed, breastmilk or formula in dim room. Brush teeth.
7:00 PMBedtime, into cot awake but drowsy. White noise, dark room.
Wake windows: ~3h / 3.25h / 3.5h. Total day sleep: 2–3h. Total night: 10–11.5h. Adjust based on your baby's wake time.

At 10 months, your baby is likely having three solid meals a day plus two to three milk feeds. Milk still provides key nutrition, don't drop feeds without checking with your GP or child health nurse.

If naps are short: Try stretching the wake window by 15 minutes. A baby napping only 30–40 minutes often needs more awake time. If no improvement after 3–4 days, they may be overtired, pull the nap earlier by 15 minutes instead.

The 10-Month Sleep Regression

The 10-month sleep regression is real, driven by motor milestones (crawling, pulling to stand, cruising), a language burst, and a fresh wave of separation anxiety. Unlike the 4-month regression which was neurological, this one is behavioural, your baby is too excited about new skills to switch off.

Signs you're in the 10-month regression:

  • Standing in the cot, your baby pulls up and can't get back down
  • Night waking with party vibes, awake at 2 AM, babbling, cruising
  • Fighting naps and clinginess at bedtime

This regression typically lasts 1–3 weeks. Don't introduce new sleep associations. Respond with brief comfort, a quick cuddle, a calm lay-back-down, a pat, but keep lights off and voice low. Give lots of daytime floor time for practice.

Separation anxiety tip: Play peek-a-boo and hide-and-seek during the day. These games teach your baby that you disappear and come back, the exact lesson they need for sleep. A consistent bedtime routine also helps your baby feel secure.

11-Month Sleep Schedule: Wake Windows & Naps

At 11 months, wake windows extend to 3.25–3.75 hours. Your baby can handle more awake time but still needs two solid naps. This is the age where the morning nap often needs capping to preserve the afternoon nap.

Sample 11-Month Sleep Schedule (2 Naps)
6:30–7:00 AMWake up, morning feed. Open curtains, natural light.
7:30 AMBreakfast, solids. Finger foods practice.
9:45–10:00 AMMorning nap, cap at 45–60 min if afternoon nap struggles. Otherwise 60–90 min.
11:00–11:30 AMWake, feed, breastmilk or formula, then active play.
12:00 PMLunch, solids, three meals a day.
2:30–3:00 PMAfternoon nap, 60–90 minutes. Must end by 4:00 PM.
4:00 PMWake, snack, small snack if hungry, then floor play.
5:30 PMDinner, solids. Family meal if possible.
6:15 PMBath, quiet play, books, consistent wind-down routine.
6:45 PMFinal feed, breastmilk or formula. Teeth brushed.
7:00–7:30 PMBedtime, into cot awake. Dark room, white noise.
Wake windows: ~3.25h / 3.5h / 3.75h. Total day sleep: 2–2.5h. Morning nap capped to protect afternoon nap.

At 11 months, the afternoon nap is often the harder one. If your baby fights it, check the morning nap length first, cap it before changing anything else.

The 11-Month Sleep Regression

Three things collide at 11 months: walking or cruising (your baby is getting vertical and mobile), a language explosion (understanding commands, maybe saying a few words), and separation anxiety round two.

Signs you're in the 11-month regression:

  • Cot gymnastics at 3 AM, wakes, pulls to stand, cruises rails, refuses to lie down
  • Bedtime battles and nap refusal, screaming when you leave, fighting the afternoon nap
  • Waking at 4:30–5:00 AM, ready to start the day

Lasts 1–3 weeks. Stay consistent, go in, lie them down with a quiet "it's sleep time," give a quick pat, and leave. Lots of daytime practice helps reduce the night-time drive to practise.

Is My Baby Ready for One Nap? The 2-to-1 Nap Transition

This is the most common question from parents of 10–12 month olds. For most babies, 11 months is too early. The 2-to-1 nap transition typically happens between 13–18 months, according to Tresillian and Karitane. What looks like one-nap readiness at this age is more often a regression, a schedule tweak, or teething.

Genuine signs of one-nap readiness (not just regression):

  • Consistent morning nap refusal for 2+ weeks
  • Morning nap extends to 2+ hours and afternoon nap is completely refused
  • Bedtime consistently past 8:30 PM even when naps are capped
  • Can manage a 4.5–5 hour morning wake window without falling apart

If your 11-month-old is fighting naps but doesn't meet these criteria, try capping the morning nap at 45–60 minutes to preserve the afternoon nap. If your baby genuinely meets the criteria at 12 months, shift the single nap to 11:30 AM–12:00 PM, aim for 2–2.5 hours, and bring bedtime forward to 6:30 PM. See the Toddler Sleep Guide →

12-Month Sleep Schedule: Wake Windows & Routine

At 12 months, most babies are still on two naps with wake windows of 3.5–4 hours. Some are beginning the shift toward one nap, if your 12-month-old is fighting the second nap consistently and meeting the readiness signs above, start the transition cautiously. Most 12-month-olds still do best on two naps.

Typical 12-month sleep needs:

  • Wake windows: 3.5–4 hours | Total sleep: 12–14 hours
  • Naps: 2 naps (60–90 min each) or starting the 2-to-1 transition
  • Night feeds: 0, most 12-month-olds don't need night feeds nutritionally

At 12 months, milk feeds typically drop to 2–3 per day. If your baby is still waking for night feeds, it's likely habit rather than hunger, check with your GP or child health nurse before night-weaning.

The 12-month sleep regression can hit around the first birthday, driven by walking, language development, and separation anxiety. It usually lasts 1–3 weeks. Stay consistent, keep night interactions brief, and ride it out.

First birthday sleep tips: Schedule the party for late morning or early afternoon to avoid derailing nap time. One disrupted nap is fine, two plus an overtired baby at bedtime is a recipe for a rough night. If they stay up late celebrating? Worth it. Just get back on schedule the next day.

When They Practise in the Cot

Finding your baby standing in the cot at 2 AM, grinning and practising new skills, is one of the most frustrating things at this age. They're not crying, they're just busy. This is normal. Your baby's brain is prioritising motor development over sleep.

  • Lots of daytime practice, floor play, cruising, push toys
  • Practise sitting down from standing, many babies get stuck standing
  • Keep night responses brief, lie them down, say "it's sleep time," leave

If your baby is standing and distressed, help them lie down with a quick pat. But if they're happily chatting, leave them be, intervening reinforces it. This phase usually passes within a couple of weeks.

Safe Sleep at 10–12 Months (Red Nose Australia)

Safe sleep doesn't stop being important after the newborn stage. Here's what Red Nose Australia recommends:

  • Lower the cot mattress to the lowest setting as soon as your baby can sit or pull to stand
  • No cot bumpers, pillows, doonas, or soft toys, suffocation and strangulation hazards. A firm, flat mattress with a fitted sheet is all your baby needs
  • Remove mobiles and hanging toys once your baby can reach them
  • Cot meets Australian safety standards, AS/NZS 2172 for portable cots, AS/NZS 2195 for household cots
  • Always place your baby on their back at the start of every sleep. By 10–12 months, if they roll onto their tummy themselves and the cot is clear, you don't need to roll them back
  • A baby sleeping bag with appropriate TOG rating is the safest bedding, it can't ride up over the face. See our Room Temperature & How to Dress Baby Guide
  • Most children sleep better in a cot until at least 2.5–3 years. The cot-to-bed transition is about safety, not age

Frequently Asked Questions

How much sleep does a 10-month-old need?

A 10-month-old typically needs 12–14 hours total: 10–12 hours at night and 2–3 hours across two naps. Tresillian recommends watching your baby's mood rather than fixating on exact numbers.

What is a good bedtime for a 10-month-old?

A good bedtime is between 6:30 PM and 7:30 PM, assuming a 6:00–7:00 AM wake time and two naps. If naps are short, bring bedtime as early as 6:00 PM, an earlier bedtime often improves night sleep.

Is the 11-month sleep regression real and how long does it last?

Yes, driven by walking/cruising, a language explosion, and separation anxiety. It typically lasts 1–3 weeks. Stay consistent and don't bring your baby into your bed, habits formed during a regression outlast it.

How long does the 12-month sleep regression last?

The 12-month regression typically lasts 1–3 weeks, driven by walking, language, and separation anxiety. It can also be the first sign of the 2-to-1 nap transition. Keep your routine consistent.
